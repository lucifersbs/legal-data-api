<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Houston's weather patterns contribute significantly to slip and fall accidents. The city's frequent heavy rainfall, high humidity, and occasional freezing conditions create hazardous walking surfaces. Property owners must take reasonable steps to address these weather-related risks, including prompt cleanup of tracked-in water, proper drainage maintenance, and application of de-icing agents during rare winter storms.</p>

Oil and chemical facilities, refineries, warehouses, and manufacturing plants must maintain rigorous safety standards. Visitors to these facilities and workers alike face risks from industrial spills, uneven surfaces, and maintenance-related hazards.</p>

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>High-Traffic Retail and Entertainment Venues</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>From the sprawling Galleria to downtown's revitalized entertainment districts, Houston's commercial areas attract millions of visitors annually. Busy restaurants, shopping centers, hotels, and entertainment venues must maintain safe conditions despite heavy foot traffic.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>Texas Premises Liability Law</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Texas premises liability law categorizes visitors and establishes different levels of care owed by property owners:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Invitees</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Customers, clients, and others invited onto the property for business purposes are owed the highest duty of care. Property owners must inspect for dangers, repair known hazards, and warn of dangerous conditions that are not open and obvious.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Licensees</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Social guests and others with permission to be on the property are entitled to warnings of known dangerous conditions that are not open and obvious.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>Trespassers</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Property owners generally owe no duty to adult trespassers except to refrain from willful, wanton, or intentional conduct. Special rules apply to child trespassers under the attractive nuisance doctrine.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading -->
<h2>Proving a Slip and Fall Case in Houston</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Texas law requires plaintiffs to prove that property owners had actual or constructive knowledge of the dangerous condition that caused their fall. Your Houston slip and fall attorney must demonstrate:</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul>
<li>A dangerous condition existed on the property</li>
<li>The property owner knew or should have known about the condition</li>
<li>The owner failed to take reasonable steps to eliminate the danger or provide adequate warning</li>
<li>The dangerous condition caused your injuries</li>
<li>You suffered damages as a result</li>
</ul>
<!-- /wp:list -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Texas follows a modified comparative negligence rule with a 51% bar. You can recover damages if you were 50% or less at fault for your accident, but your recovery will be reduced by your percentage of fault. If you're found 51% or more at fault, you cannot recover any damages.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<h2>Steps to Take After a Houston Slip and Fall</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:list -->
<ul>
<li><strong>Seek immediate medical attention:</strong> Document all injuries, even those that seem minor at first.</li>
<li><strong>Report the accident:</strong> Notify the property owner, manager, or security and request a written incident report.</li>
<li><strong>Document the scene:</strong> Take photos of the hazard, surrounding area, warning signs (or lack thereof), and your injuries.</li>
<li><strong>Collect witness information:</strong> Get contact details from anyone who observed your fall.</li>
<li><strong>Preserve evidence:</strong> Keep the shoes and clothing you were wearing.</li>
<li><strong>Contact an attorney:</strong> Reach out to a slip and fall attorney Houston residents recommend.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Frequently Asked Questions</h2>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>How long do I have to file a slip and fall lawsuit in Texas?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Texas has a two-year statute of limitations for most slip and fall cases, meaning you must file your lawsuit within two years of the accident date. However, if your claim is against a government entity, you may have much shorter notice requirements. Contact an attorney immediately to protect your rights.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>What if I fell at work in Houston?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Workplace falls are generally covered by workers' compensation, which provides limited benefits regardless of fault. However, if a third party (such as a property owner, contractor, or equipment manufacturer) contributed to your fall, you may have a separate personal injury claim.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>How much does a Houston slip and fall attorney cost?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Most Houston slip and fall attorneys work on a contingency fee basis, meaning you pay nothing upfront. The attorney receives a percentage of your settlement or court award—typically 33-40%. If your attorney doesn't recover compensation, you owe no attorney fees.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->

<!-- wp:heading {"level":3} -->
<h3>What if the property owner claims they didn't know about the hazard?</h3>
<!-- /wp:heading -->

<!-- wp:paragraph -->
<p>Property owners can be held liable if they should have known about a dangerous condition through reasonable inspection and maintenance. Your attorney will investigate how long the hazard existed and whether the owner failed to meet their duty of care.</p>
<!-- /wp:paragraph -->
